Tyler HerroBucks player

Tyler Herro describes how he handles defensive pressure and uses his gravity on the court to create opportunities for his teammates.

“I get a lot of attention throughout the past few years. Guys pick me up early, whether it's full court or right when I cross half court, guys are picking me up. I've been seeing the best defender on the other team for three or four years now. I'm going to have a gravity to my game that is going to open up other things for other guys. I think just being able to counteract that, my best years I've had were on the ball, but I can do a little bit of both.”

Tyler HerroBucks player

Tyler Herro shares his impressions of the new head coach's urgent and demanding coaching style based on his previous experience in Memphis.

“He's urgent. He's a guy that wants things done and he wants them done the right way. I kind of got the sense of that, how he had his Memphis teams playing, picking up full court, putting pressure on teams, playing fast. He's a tough-nosed guy. He's really urgent, like I said, and he wants things done the right way. That's the beauty of being here with a bunch of young guys that he's continuing to push us, no matter what, he expects us to continue to get better every day.”

Taylor JenkinsBucks coach

Taylor Jenkins discusses his strategy for utilizing Tyler Herro as a primary ball handler and managing his role within the offense.

“I wanted to watch him in this system a little bit more. Ryan, with the ding and some of the fouls, maybe just did not get the rhythm that we were hoping. Hopefully the next opportunity we get more of what we are hoping to see. Scoot as well, willing moving the ball, but also can be great with the ball in his hands. When there were opportunities in dead-ball situations, I wanted to get the ball in Tyler Herro's hands, see him handle pressure, how we are going to have to calibrate when teams pick him up full court and are trying to pressure him even in the half court to deny his catch. That was something I wanted to lean in, knowing that Ryan, Scoot, even challenging Brain to be guys that bring the ball up the floor, but I do not want Tyler just to be a guy that is playing off the ball.”

On Tyler Herro

Tyler Herro discusses the importance of translating the intensity shown in practice sessions into actual game performance during the preseason.

“I think just translating it from practice to the games is what he means. In practice, we are obviously going against each other and we're competing hard and we're trying to make each other better, iron sharpening iron. At the end of the day, it is preseason, but we have to find a way to translate what we're doing in practice to the games. Having a little bit more urgency, a little bit more care factor, that's what we've been talking about, just knowing it's preseason but knowing that the real thing starts in the next few days.”

Taylor JenkinsBucks coach

Taylor Jenkins discusses the team's offensive development and how players are learning to handle defensive pressure.

“We're going to face pressure regardless of who it's going to be. All of our guys have to develop against that style of defense. When you talk about Ryan, Scoot, Gary, you've got Braden, Cas, you even throw Cam in there, you got Tyler, it's going to be different ways that we're going to have to attack pressure. Learning their tendencies, if it's their ability to break down the defense one-on-one, it's playing off of get actions, which we call snap, it's single pick-and-rolls, double pick-and-rolls, it's north-south, east-west, trying to get a feel for one or running habits in our initial shape, which is still a work in progress.”

On Ryan, Scoot, Gary Trent Jr., Braden, Cas, Cam, Tyler Herro
