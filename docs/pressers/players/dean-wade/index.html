Dean Wade76ers player

Dean Wade explains how he developed his defensive identity early in his NBA career to secure a spot in the league.

“I think a little bit of both. Kansas State when I was there, I think we were like a top-five defensive team in the nation and we played the same way we switched everything. I had to learn that pretty fast when I was at Kansas State. I was not really diving into my defensive ability then too much. I just did it good enough to get stops. In the NBA, it was a way to stick in the league. I was a two-way, I was undrafted. I had to figure out a way to kind of separate myself. I had great coaches when I was young in the NBA, a guy named Josh Broghammer and Chris Darnell were my coaches and they were counting on me. We sat down one time and they were like, if you want to stay in this league, every team has a scorer and a shooter, every team has somebody that can create. Not every team has a defender. You need to move good enough laterally, that is where you can stick if you want to stick in this league. So I took that conversation and I dove in headfirst into that.”

Dean Wade76ers player

Dean Wade discusses how impressed he is by the high basketball IQ of his new 76ers teammates during training camp.

“It is completely different. You know their tendencies playing against them, but when you are on the same team, you can pick their brain. My biggest thing is the way they think the game is the most impressive thing. Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, everyone, the way they think the game is at such a high level. As an NBA player, you think you know the game well, and then you get on a team like this and it is night and day. You see the way they think about plays, think about defenses, the way they think about rotations. It is similar to some things I thought, but they go a little more in-depth and it is super impressive.”

On Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown

Dean Wade explains that his coaches are encouraging him to be more aggressive and shoot the ball whenever he is open.

Dean Wade reflects on the versatility of the 76ers roster and the potential for complex defensive schemes.

“It is crazy. We have so many vets on this team who have seen so many different things and played completely different styles of basketball. I am interested to see what we stick to or what we try. It is pretty impressive we have so many versatile defenders, athletes. We have so many good players that can do different things. Dean Wade76ers player

Dean Wade highlights the 76ers' potential to be an elite transition offense due to the speed and playmaking of his teammates.

“We have so many great players, so many unstoppable people in transition. It is almost like a free throw or basket in transition. I think a big emphasis is getting out more in transition. The way our defense is, how many turnovers we can create, we should be unbelievable in transition. Especially with Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey, all those guys, they get out in transition, those are hard people to stop. When you are out in transition, it makes everything a little bit easier. Your decision-making can be easier, you are going to see the open guy a little easier.”

On Jaylen Brown, Tyrese Maxey

Dean Wade76ers player

Dean Wade praises the decision-making skills of Tyrese Maxey and Jaylen Brown during transition play.

“They are unbelievable. They are so fast and quick. Tyrese Maxey, he gets out in transition and it is unbelievable how fast he moves, how fast he can change direction. I feel like the thing that really surprised me the most is they never make the wrong decision, they are always making the right decision. If that is dashing it out on the baseline, kicking out, scoring, whatever it is, they always make the right decision. That is hard, especially for a young guy. Tyrese Maxey is only in his second year, so seeing him, he is playing like he is a vet. He knows the right plays and he is doing it and he is not forcing anything.”

On Tyrese Maxey, Jaylen Brown

Dean Wade discusses his personal relationship with executive Mike Gansey despite his change in professional title.

Dean Wade notes the similarities in defensive mindset between himself and his new teammates Jaylen Brown and Tyrese Maxey.

“There is a lot. The past few days, especially with Jaylen Brown and Tyrese Maxey, the way they think the game is similar to the way I think the game, especially on the defensive end. When I see early, the past couple weeks, with Tyrese Maxey and those guys, I love the way he plays. I really do.
