**1. Mahershala Ali — "I would love for him to stay and I would love for him to do something like what Magic and Larry Bird did." — Mahershala Ali on Steph Curry finishing his career with the Warriors** [02:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N534ZVbQQDs&t=140s)

Mahershala Ali: "I would hope we could have both. But I'm a fan, so I don't know how realistic fans are all the time. But at the end of the day, if Steph wants to be there, I would love for him to stay and I would love for him to do something like what Magic and Larry Bird did, some of those brothers who were able to stay with the team and be defined by that in some ways. But at this point, clearly Steph has earned the right to do whatever he wants to do. He doesn't owe us anything per se, but it's been beautiful getting to watch his career unfold and play out. I was really worried early on with all the ankle injuries and what not. You weren't sure if we were going to have him on the court like that, but considering he was able to get past that and be relatively healthy for years, yeah, I would love to see him stay."

**2. Carmelo Anthony — "When black actors get Oscars, the preeminent notion around fans is like, 'All right, you are now validated.'" — Carmelo Anthony and Mahershala Ali on the validation of winning an Oscar** [02:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ko6ThLsvJNc&t=144s)

**Carmelo Anthony:** "Is it different being a black actor though? Because I feel like once, especially, I remember when Denzel got his, when Halle got hers, **Michael B. Jordan** just got his. Like when black actors get Oscars, the preeminent notion around fans is like, 'All right, you are now officially on this list of people. You are now validated. You are in the Denzel class. You are in the class of A-list tier dudes.' And you're saying that it's kind of give and take. It's good but it's not."

**Mahershala Ali:** "I just don't know what that means. I respectfully, meaning it's kind of just talk. You know, it's just it doesn't translate to anything real. It's just who's better, LeBron or Jordan? Who cares? They're great. There's no definitive answer. You know what I'm saying? So I think, and there's something to like, it's not the same world when Sydney Poitier won an Oscar as when **Halle Berry** or Viola Davis or, you know what I mean? It's different eras, different world."

**2. Mahershala Ali — "I think COVID, even maybe even more than the NIL, has really shifted college sports in terms of the eligibility." — Mahershala Ali on how NIL and COVID changed college basketball** [05:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N534ZVbQQDs&t=303s)

Mahershala Ali: "I think we're in a very different time and we're still trying to acclimate in terms of how to think about it. I think COVID, even maybe even more than the NIL, has really shifted college sports in terms of the eligibility factors that weighed in where therefore kids coming out of high school or even JC are having a more difficult time getting scholarships now. People being able to stay longer because now they're getting paid to be in school. So I think between the combination of COVID and the NIL opportunities, it's made college basketball even more competitive in terms of the spots. I think the thing that college had going for it is the very thing that a lot of us would call BS, but it also is what college ball had going for it in terms of camaraderie and doing it for something bigger than yourself, learning about sacrifice, the pride, loving the school, your teammates and what not, before you end up getting hit with all the, this is how you take care of yourself and this is how you become the best player, this is how you have longevity and all that."
