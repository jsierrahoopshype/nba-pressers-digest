**2. Dusty May — "he's got the size, the skill set, and so I think it's just a matter of him getting more comfortable" — Dusty May on Darryn Peterson's adjustment to the college game** [00:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jusQdayEPM4&t=51s)

Dusty May: "**Darryn Peterson** is obviously being a new player, we tend to have a tendency to overreact every game, good or bad, and especially for players who are playing in college basketball this time last year, playing two games a week and maybe one of them's a money game. And so, a good game, a bad game, we just have a tendency to overreact. With Darryn, the talent is obvious, he's got the size, the skill set, and so I think it's just a matter of him getting more comfortable playing, I'm sure getting used to teammates. They've had a lot of injuries where guys are coming in and out of the lineup and probably without Markkanen maybe they're playing a little bit more through their guards."

**4. Dusty May — "I do think the chip on the shoulder is real" — Dusty May on Walter Clayton Jr.'s development and mindset** [02:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jusQdayEPM4&t=146s)

Dusty May: "I do think the chip on the shoulder is real. He went to a program at Iona that probably helped him a lot as well. Walter was always skilled, he was always a good player. I don't think he had the intensity and the tenacity that he got there playing for Coach Pitino. And then he went to Florida and it was another perfect situation for him where he was able to step in, play kind of an off-guard role his first year, and then evolve into a primary handler, which I think really helped his NBA career and his long-term potential as well. He just has a real belief and confidence in himself."

**5. Dusty May — "our guards now are starting to rebound" — Dusty May on the team's improved defensive rebounding** [04:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jusQdayEPM4&t=245s)

Dusty May: "Part of that is our guards now are starting to rebound. If you watch this in the Cal and you watch this the first couple games, our guards act as if it wasn't their job to help rebound and it wasn't their responsibility. And now, I can think of five or six plays last night, and it probably was the difference in the game where they weren't 50-50 basketballs, they were 80-20, 90-10, and we had a guard go in and just get in the mix and maybe get a fingertip on it. And then when our guards can come up with those loose balls, it's a broken floor and we're making better decisions in transition as well."

**6. Dusty May — "I was just going to go be a student manager at Indiana" — Dusty May on his decision to attend Indiana University** [05:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jusQdayEPM4&t=321s)

Dusty May: "My junior year of high school I had a good junior year and I was being recruited by a lot of small schools, Division II and NAIs, and then I had a bad senior season. And at that point I was a little frustrated, I was just going to go be a student manager at Indiana. I went through the process of applying to school and started the introduction with a side story with the team doctor in Indiana. At the last minute I got an itch to play, I went to a Division II school and I wasn't there very long and I realized that wasn't going to get me where I wanted to get to as far as coaching, and I thought if I go work for Coach Knight I could probably get a varsity coaching job in Indiana by 25 by my mid-20s and start my coaching career."
