**1. Erik Spoelstra — "I'm just glad to see him have that type of game." — Bam Adebayo's performance and impact on the team's offense** [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EDm4RjjbnyQ&t=0s)

Erik Spoelstra: "It was almost like a old-school game where we were playing at the high post. A lot of it was in the post or in pick-and-rolls. But the way they are defending it, we were just letting him try to create an action, get to the paint and then play out of whatever paint touch happened from there. He made some really good passes, set up the guys pretty consistently throughout the course of the game. I was glad to see him have the triple-double because he was playing the right way in the entire game. Super efficient, and I think it just fed confidence into the other guys. We needed a bunch of guys to step up in this game, have Bam out, and I think the way Bam played set that up for everybody."

**5. Erik Spoelstra — "He faces the defense a lot more now, so he's able to see what's happening." — Bam Adebayo's development in reading double teams** [03:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EDm4RjjbnyQ&t=209s)

Erik Spoelstra: "Well, he faces the defense a lot more now, so he's able to see what's happening and where the help is coming from. They were bringing it from the baseline, and then if he put the ball on the floor, they would aggressively dig or trap from the top. So I think the first step is facing it. If you're backing in, sometimes you can get surprised. I also think we spent an inordinate amount of time this year post-spacing because of the dynamic between Giannis and Bam. And if we're not right with our spacing when the two of them are on the court, we get jammed up. It's been a while since we've been jammed up, and I'm thinking about that Charlotte game, it was crazy. But that was part of the process we had to go through to work through this. And so we spend a lot of time with it, we watch a lot of film on it, Dave worked on it, and then that helps you, even in a game like this where Giannis is not playing, everybody's spacing was a lot more detailed, and we were able to generate offense from the post-up, and that's progress from our work, really, through the skill."

**6. Erik Spoelstra — "I didn't want to lose the 3.9. If you're playing from a position of power." — The strategy behind small-ball lineups and spacing** [04:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EDm4RjjbnyQ&t=289s)

Erik Spoelstra: "A lot of it was because in the second quarter, they went to that super small lineup, and then that's when they were actually getting us scrambled around, and I didn't want to lose the 3.9. If you're playing from a position of power and playing with a lead, and they're scrambling you, then giving up the 3.9, well, that's why we went to some of those lineups and were able to flatten them out, and those were our switchable lineups. On the other end, I don't have a lot of complaints offensively. I'm not sure what you're referring to. Yeah, sometimes we could have tested the paint a little bit more. If I was to say anything about our offense tonight, we probably could have scored 135, we were throwing it into the stands. We could have kept it simple a little bit more. I see it as a product of the guys having fun, and they're trying to make somebody else get an open shot, and there's a little bit of flair for the moment. I'm not totally upset at that, especially from where we were a month ago, but if we can just get some more shots on goal, I also like scoring 135, I'm fine with 135 and 140."

**7. Erik Spoelstra — "It's the ground-and-pound mentality that both of those guys have." — Bam Adebayo's aggressive mentality and decision-making** [06:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EDm4RjjbnyQ&t=382s)

Erik Spoelstra: "With Bam, it's the ground-and-pound mentality that both of those guys have. And I think some of that of how Bam plays is rubbing off on Bam. I think he's being more assertive once he gets into the circle area, just being way more aggressive, even sometimes just trying to go up and dunk it, and that's when he gets hammered instead of trying to finesse it. But all of it, and I think once you start seeing it happen more often, then you start going to it more often. We've been emphasizing, you know, with the two of them, it should feel like a frontcourt when they're leaving the game after the game. It should feel like this an element of ground-and-pound and physicality where they have to ice themselves. If we're not doing that, then we're not maximizing their brutality. And they can do it in different ways. It's not just putting your head down, it's playing with nuance to get to your brutality, and I think those two are getting better with that."
